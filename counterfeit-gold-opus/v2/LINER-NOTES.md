# FERRYMAN TAKES FAKES (V2) · liner notes

*The band, the family, the label, the funeral and both recordings below are invented. Every sound was composed,
performed and printed inside DaemonV12.*

## What was in the box

The Velvet Hearse made one LP, *Music for the Viewing Room* (Lazarus Sound Library LSL-019, 1971): the house combo of
Vane & Sons Funeral Home, playing quietly for wakes and failing. Side B, track 3, "Two Coins for the Ferryman", is
where the first beat came from.

The song was written for one funeral. Silas Vane, who founded the firm, left instructions to be buried with two gold
coins for the ferryman. The family put two brass ones in his hand, painted gold. The band played him to the cemetery
and played him loose on the way home, the way funeral bands do. Lazarus's engineer had left a portable recorder
running on the hearse.

That reel was in the LP's master box, labelled in pencil "PROCESSION". It has never been released. It is the LP's song
played outdoors, with no Rhodes and no vibraphone:

- Lucius Vane carries a bell lyre.
- The Deacon's bass is strapped to a pram and played so hard it blats like a tuba.
- Iggy plays a parade snare that never stops talking, and a log for a cowbell.
- Two mourners sing back at Odile.
- Brother Ezra plays the one riff the LP never had.

## Side A, track 1: FERRYMAN TAKES FAKES (V2)

88 BPM. D minor, and D7 when the street band is playing. 2:32.

The first beat had one record. This one has three: the LP, the Procession Take, and the first beat itself, now an old
record in the same crate. Both 1971 recordings play at 0.840896 of their speed, so F becomes D and each of their beats
becomes one beat here. V1 plays at its own speed, or at half: still D minor, an octave down, in half time.
