# HOOK//10 listening guide

Four versions of one 10-second ident. D minor, 96 BPM, four bars, exactly 10.000 s, all at −16 LUFS. Same four
instruments in each: Impossible Voice (lead), Living Bass, mallet bars, escapement clock. No drums.

**Easiest way to listen (Android):** open the listening page and tap a version. It loops without a gap, and tapping
another version switches at the same point in the bar. "Hide names" shuffles them for a blind pass.
<https://claude.ai/artifact/YPPhSu2A4S6DESMNt3po6q> (private to the owner until shared.)

**Files** (in [`hook10/`](hook10)): `HOOK10-<version>.wav` is the seamless master. `…-loop-x3.mp3` plays it three
times so you hear the return to bar 1. `….mp3` is a single pass.

## The versions

| | Workflow | What happens | Chords, bar by bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Existing: `notes` and `repeats` | The hook three times, unchanged. Bar 4 is a written turnaround. Only the timbre moves. | Dm · Dm · Dm · Dm A7 |
| **B1** | score/2 motifs | The same hook. Bar 2 answers it a third lower. Bar 3 breaks off its rising sixth and climbs with it while the mallets play that fragment twice as fast and the clock doubles. Bar 4 turns the hook upside down to close. | Dm · B♭ · Gm C · Dm A7 |
| **B2** | score/2 motifs | A different hook: three repeated notes, a leap, a reach up. Voice and bass share its rhythm; the mallets answer every call at double speed. Bar 3 turns to D major and the clock drops to half time. Bar 4 is the hook upside down. | Dm · B♭ C · D · Gm A7 |
| **B3** | score/2 motifs | The B1 hook. Bar 2 is its mirror image. Bars 3–4 sing it at half speed with a small melisma on the long note, while the mallets keep playing it at full speed. | Dm · F · B♭ · C A7 |

**A and B1 are the matched pair.** Bars 1 and 4 are identical note for note. Instruments, gains, room, macros,
expression curves, seed and loudness are the same. Only bars 2 and 3 differ. B2 and B3 are extra candidates that
share the palette and loudness.

In every version the last chord is A7 and the first is D minor, so the cadence resolves across the loop point.

## What to listen for

1. **Memorability.** Stop the player. Which hook can you hum back?
2. **Written or looped.** Which sounds composed, and which sounds like a loop?
3. **Alive.** Which performance feels more expressive?
4. **The return.** Does bar 4 pull you back into bar 1? Is the loop point audible at all?
5. Ten minutes later, without replaying: which one do you still have?

Specific moments: the cry on the second note of the hook (0.5 s). In B1, the mallets racing the voice in bar 3 (5.0 s).
In B2, the turn to major at 5.0 s. In B3, the long high note with its upper-neighbour turn at about 6 s.

## What nobody should conclude yet

I composed all four and cannot hear them. A and B were written by the same agent in one session, knowing which was
which. That is not the blind protocol the spec asks for (three independent runs per condition, at least three
listeners, 27 pooled choices, B needing 19 or more on memorability and on "composed"). Until people have listened,
the only supported claims are structural: B1–B3 pass the symbolic gate, and all four pass the technical gate.
