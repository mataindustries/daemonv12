# PARALLAX — production log

## Six-line musical thesis

1. Hook: a displaced B4→E5 fourth, falling D5→B4→F#4 answer, then G4→B4→C#5→B4.
2. Rhythm: 140 BPM, half-time backbeat; asymmetric kick cells and deliberately late offbeat hats.
3. Low end: ABYSS alone owns sustained sub; E2 in the opening gives way to E1 at the return. Kicks release before the bass answers.
4. Contrast: the pulse dissolves into a bassless, long-vowel suspension and a clipped leading tone.
5. Payoff: the learned hook returns through PHANTOM, with SIGNAL replying below it and REACTOR answering the sub in the midrange.
6. Ending: the hook contracts into E5→E4 over an E minor-sixth colour, with a composed decay inside 28 seconds.

## Risk Pass — one decision, after competence

The impact then catches the E5 leap instead of doubling the expected downbeat. This could make the return recognizable through timing as well as timbre.

**Upside / failure:** a brief exposed vocal pickup may become a memorable physical catch; it could also make the downbeat feel late or weaken the return.

**Minimum evidence and cost:** one score edit and one complete render, compared with V3's onset map, native analysis and existing section review. No new analysis tools. Human preference remains unknown.

**Keep / revert criterion:** provisionally keep only if the original melody is unchanged, the half-beat catch has an authored bass/kick gap, subsequent backbeat/grid remains clear on the score, delivery stays clean, and the complete ending survives. Revert if the change causes collision, erased hook events or a technically compromised arrival. This is a provisional compositional decision, not a fabricated listening result.
