# STILL HERE — musical theses (written before V1)

Two competing 12-second scores for a DaemonV12 aerospace spec film. Both put ignition on
audio frame 176,400 (4.000 s, picture frame 240 at 60 fps) and end at frame 529,200.

The film's three checkpoints shape both pieces:

- **4 s:** the factory comes alive.
- **6 s:** a musical idea worth remembering.
- **12 s:** inspiration.

Previous reviews said what to avoid: a slow, predictable start, and too little after the
activation. Both pieces therefore start with a fragment of the hook in the first second.
Neither waits until after ignition to start moving.

| | A — PRECISION IGNITION | B — THE HUMAN SPARK |
|---|---|---|
| Grid | 120 BPM, 4/4, 6 bars of 2 s | 180 BPM beat (1/3 s), 4/4, 9 bars of 1.333 s |
| Pulse before ignition | press thump on every half note (1 Hz), *on* the grid | machine breathes in 3 (one press per second) *against* the spark's 4-beat cells |
| Tonal world | A major with a Mixolydian edge; I – IV – ♭VI – ♭VII – I | D major with a Lydian II (E/D); I – II – vi – IV–V – vi–V⁶ – I |
| Hook carrier | bass statement + talkbox answer (two instruments) | one rising line that grows note by note |
| Rhythm signature | tresillo 3+3+2, repeated in call and answer | quarter · eighth · pushed dotted-quarter · quarter |
| Groove | four-on-the-floor under syncopated lines | half-time with rolling 16ths, double-time in the ascent |

## A — PRECISION IGNITION

1. **Hook.**
   - **Call:** the Tempo String states **A–E–A** (1–5–8, bare fifths: the machine).
   - **Answer:** a talkbox voice replies **C♯–E–F♯** (3–5–6: the human third, ending on the
     open sixth).
   - **Rhythm:** both cells use the same 3+3+2 sixteenth rhythm.
   - **Six notes in all:** one rising gesture, handed from the low instrument to the voice.
2. **Rhythm / pacing.**
   - **Before ignition:** the press hits every half note (1 Hz). Cable ticks accent the
     3+3+2 so the hook's rhythm is already present.
   - **After ignition:** a four-on-the-floor membrane kick under the syncopated lines.
     Snare on 2 and 4. A cable-scrape off-beat.
3. **Low end.**
   - **Owner:** the cable's A1 (55 Hz) owns the bass. Its sub ducks under each kick.
   - **Rests:** the bass rests while the voice answers, which gives the call and response
     its space.
4. **Contrast.**
   - **0–3 s:** a muted, dark statement at 0 s, answered by a tiny glass blip at 1 s. The
     voice hums its answer at 2.5 s.
   - **3 s:** the cable is wound from rhythm into pitch, a turbine spooling up.
   - **3.875 s:** the hand grabs it, and 125 ms of breath follows.
   - **4.000 s:** full spectrum, kick, crash plate and brass stab.
5. **Payoff.**
   - **Development:** the answer cell climbs by sequence over 4–10 s (C♯4 → F♯4 → A4 → B4).
   - **The merge:** in the ascent (♭VI–♭VII, F–G), call and answer merge into one rhythm,
     with bass and voice in unison.
   - **10.0 s:** the hook returns over A as C♯5–E5–F♯5. The open sixth finally resolves up
     to the octave, **A5 at 11.0 s**: the answer is completed.
6. **Ending.**
   - **11.0 s:** the final A(add9) hits and rings, while the precision ticks keep running
     quietly.
   - **11.75 s:** a servo "lock" click, a callback to the opening press that invites replay.
   - **Narration space:** the last second keeps the speech band clear.

**Emotional progression:** dormant precision → coiled anticipation → confident conversation
(machine states, human answers warmly) → collective climb → the human answer completes the
machine's phrase.

## B — THE HUMAN SPARK

1. **Hook: the spark.**
   - **Notes:** **D–E–A–B (→ D′)**: step, a pushed fourth (the spark), step, then the octave.
   - **Rhythm:** quarter, eighth, dotted quarter on the "and" of 2, quarter.
   - **It grows:** three notes, then four, then four with a hummed voice.
   - **It completes at ignition:** the voice cries the fifth note, D5, on 4.000 s.
2. **Rhythm / pacing.**
   - **Before ignition:** the machine breathes in 3, one heavy press per second plus two
     light ticks. The spark repeats in 4-beat cells.
   - **The lock:** the two cycles coincide only at 0 and at 4.000 s (12 beats). Ignition is
     the moment the machine falls into the human's meter.
   - **After ignition:** half-time drums with rolling 16th ticks. In the ascent the drums go
     double-time, with a snare roll.
3. **Low end.** From ignition the bass *plays the spark* (D2–E2–A2–B2): the fragile high
   music-box idea becomes the powerful low statement. It uses a warm sub and saw, with a
   cable attack.
4. **Contrast.**
   - **Before ignition:** only a tiny high music box and a low machine; the middle is empty.
   - **At ignition:** register expansion fills the middle.
   - **5.33 s:** the Lydian II (E/D) is the wonder chord.
   - **Ascent:** a rising bass G–A–B–C♯ → D under the spark, sequenced up by steps.
5. **Payoff.**
   - **10.0 s:** the voices sing the complete spark, D4–E4–A4–B4.
   - **10.667 s:** the line lands on **D5** on the final bar line, with the music box two
     octaves above.
6. **Ending.**
   - **Final chord:** D(add9) with a Lydian G♯ shimmer, "possibility".
   - **The machine:** its 1 Hz pulse returns, now in sync. Replaying the piece brings back
     the opening.
   - **Tail:** the voice decays to 12.0 s.

**Emotional progression:** fragile curiosity → a machine waiting → the spark catches → wonder
(Lydian) → collective ascent → arrival.

## What makes them different

| | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | a dialogue between two instruments | a monologue that grows |
| Rhythm signature | tresillo | lyrical pushes |
| Time | on-grid 1 Hz pulse | 3-against-4 that locks at ignition |
| Harmony | Mixolydian ♭VI–♭VII–I | Lydian II and a rising bass |
| Ignition device | spool-up and breath | metric lock and the completed spark |
| Payoff | resolves the answer (6 → 8) | completes the line (→ octave) |

Shared on purpose: the MORPH percussion family and the Tempo String's physical character.
