# BLACKOUT//ENGINE · full-length edition

Industrial hip-hop: 128 BPM, G minor, 72 bars, **exactly 2:15.000** (5,953,500 frames). It is developed from the
approved 30-second experiment ([`../blackout-engine-001/`](../blackout-engine-001/README.md), unchanged). One
composition and arrangement pass and one bounded technical QA/fix pass; it stops here for the owner's listening
approval.

**Nobody has listened to it yet.** The composer (an AI agent) cannot hear audio. Musical descriptions below are design
intent. The numbers are signal measurements, not quality scores.

## The story in one paragraph

The approved riff is a machine playing a lament: three G strokes, an octave scream, a ratchet down the stair
G–F♯–F–E–E♭–D. The full record adds the lament's other half:

1. **The machine's second hook.** A bell melody, D–F–G | B♭–A–G, in the riff's own rhythm twice as long.
2. **The human claims it.** When the power fails, the voice sings the stair, reaches D for the first time, then starts
   the bell melody. The machine's second hook turns out to be the human's answer.
3. **The machine changes.** It comes back limping in triplets with new harmony.
4. **The climax.** Everything argues at once until the power fails again.
5. **The resolution.** Only then is the whole song sung, stair and answer, and the engine returns warm under it. The
   last cadence moves every voice down one semitone, A♭ major → G major: the riff's own A♭→G pickup turned into
   harmony, and the first B natural in the record.

- **Timbre Lab** was loaded only through its documented opt-in (`--import ./timbre-lab/register.ts`, in `build.ts`).
- **No engine or instrument changed.** `node studio-001/rack/cli.ts freeze` passes ([`freeze-check.txt`](delivery/provenance/freeze-check.txt)).
- **The approved 30-second package is unchanged.** All 31 files listed in its `SHA256SUMS` verify and git reports no
  change ([`approved-30s-unchanged.txt`](delivery/provenance/approved-30s-unchanged.txt)).
- **Not used:** Breath Reed. The singer is the human, and a second wind voice would compete with it. Clay Pot.
- **Made of:** numeric parameters and seeded noise only. No recordings, samples, SoundFonts, impulse responses,
  General MIDI or artist vocals. The voice is wordless additive synthesis.
- **Melodic material:** the descending chromatic fourth (a centuries-old lament figure) and a diatonic answer phrase.
  Neither is taken from a recording or a known song.
- **Production method:** rack stems that share an engine track (the engine takes at most 15) are summed sample for
  sample with an overflow check: bells = bar + tine + gong, machines = clock + log + spring, air = bearings. The sums
  are recorded in [`stem-groups.json`](delivery/provenance/stem-groups.json).

**Unresolved provenance items (commercial rights).** These are open, and nothing here grants a license.

1. Licensing of rendered output is pending the owner's declaration for every family used: V12 Signature, Signature Rack
   (approved for sound, not for licensing), Timbre Lab and HEADLINER.
2. Timbre Lab (Hand Skin, Hollow Log) is not owner-approved.
3. Authorship and work-for-hire status of AI-generated composition and code is to be recorded by the owner.

The master, MP3 and stems are therefore private research assets.

## Technical QA

One bounded pass. The candidate was measured, two fixes were applied, and it was re-measured. `node build.ts pre-qa`
rebuilds the candidate byte for byte (same SHA-256 as the measured render). Both records are in `provenance/`:
`qa-before-fix-pass.json` and `qa-final.json`.

**Before the QA pass (calibration, part of composing).** Three calibration renders:

- The new parts started too far back in the phone band (300 Hz–8 kHz), measured against the rest of the mix:
  - Hook B: −3.6 dB where it is introduced;
  - bowed chords: −14 dB;
  - hand drums: −26 dB.

  They were raised. The hands also got a fast compressor, because their transients limited plain gain.
- The voice compressor was relaxed (2.5:1 → 2:1) so that section levels register.
- The bells step back under the climax voice, which shares their register (D5–C6).
- The drop bass went to −3 dB (it had sat 9 dB under the cry).
- Three ghost cries planned for the escalation measured 13–14 dB under the mix wherever they were placed. They were
  removed, so the voice is first heard after the trip.

**The QA pass:**

## Things only listening can decide

- Does Hook B read as a second hook, or as decoration?
- Is the moment the voice hands the bells' melody back (0:41) a revelation or a coincidence?
- Does the triplet transformation feel like the same machine, changed, or a different song?
- Does the A♭ chord (1:15) land as menace?
- In the climax, is the counterpoint legible on a phone? There the voice leads by 2.4 dB and the riff sits 7 dB under
  the rest in the phone band.
- Does the overload trip feel like a denied drop, or like a mistake?
- Is the warm ending earned, or too sweet? Does A♭ → G major sound like resolution, or like a different record?
