## New musical material

**A second melody: the reply.** A held root leaps up a seventh, then falls through the fifth, fourth and third, on a
6 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 sixteenth rhythm. It is the hook's opposite: the hook reaches up and holds; the reply holds, then lets
go. It plays four roles:

1. a dark bass riff in C♯ minor (minor seventh);
2. a hymn in E major (major seventh);
3. the bass line under the hook in the finale;
4. a phrase in the music box at the very end.

**Counterpoint.** Played against the hook, the reply makes no semitone clash on the strong notes; I checked every
simultaneous pair. Under the hook it implies a moving bass (E – B/D♯ – A – C♯m in one bar), so the same melody gains
new harmony.

**Other new material:**

- **The Undertow.** A bass-led groove with its own drums:
  - the kick follows the bass;
  - the snare is pushed and dragged;
  - every fourth bar ends in a purposeful rest;
  - in the third cycle the Impossible Voice takes two notes of every riff, and a talking drum answers.
- **The hook implied.** In the Undertow and the Return the synth answers the bass with single pulse pairs, one per
  bar. The hook is spread across four bars and never stated whole.
- **Two new answer endings** for the hook: on the dominant G♯7 (with the leading tone B♯), and on B.
- **A four-way glitch.** One melody is passed between voice, bass, a talking drum (each pulse pair one stroke that
  bends up a sixth) and the hollow log.
- **The human afterimage.** In the hymn the sopranos follow the altos a dotted quarter behind, and the music box
  becomes a third voice. At the cadence the sopranos catch up and sing with the altos.
- **The finale's events:**
  - the melodies swap registers;
  - the choir's echo catches up with the lead through the cascade;
  - a deceptive landing on C major, then C – D – E;
  - a resolving pair at the top;
  - a bass break answered by the voice.

**Remaining trade-offs, left for listening:**

- **The bass-led grooves are narrow** (undertow −20 dB side/mid). A mono bass leading the mix is a club convention, but
  if they feel small, the lever is more stereo in the answers and percussion.
- **In the finale the reply is a supporting line** (−3.9 dB under the rest at 300 Hz–1 kHz while the hook plays). It
  leads in the undertow, the return, the swap and the bass break.
- **At the landing the lead is above the choir only in 1–4 kHz.** Below 1 kHz the C major chord belongs to the choir,
  by design.
- **The ending is quiet** (−25 LUFS), music box and hum, which makes the section contrast 12 LU. It may need a moment
  of silence before the next track, or a slightly louder music box.

## Things only listening can decide

1. Is the reply memorable by itself, as a riff in the Undertow (0:21), as a hymn (1:17) and as a bass line (1:51)?
2. Does the Undertow make you move? Do the single-pair answers make the hook feel present without being stated?
3. Does the hook over the reply in C♯ minor (0:46) feel like a meeting in the dark, and its E major return (1:51) like
   the same meeting in daylight?
4. Is the four-way glitch (1:00–1:08) coherent? Do the talking drum's bent sixths read as the melody?
5. Is the hymn tender, and does the canon sound like voices following each other rather than blur? Is its D – A – Am – E
   cadence the turning point?
6. **The landing (2:08.8):** does the C major arrival surprise you and still feel inevitable? Do C – D – E and the
   climb to E6 earn the release?
7. Does the bass break with the voice (2:21) and the stop on the first pulse pair make you want to play it again? Does
   the music box's D♯ → E end the story?
