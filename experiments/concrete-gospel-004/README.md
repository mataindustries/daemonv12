# CONCRETE GOSPEL · FULL-LENGTH EDITION

An original record in F minor at 144 BPM (half time), 76 bars: 2:07 of music plus the last chord's ring, 2:12 in all.
Its building blocks:

- **Drums and 808:** the approved Concrete Gospel V2 kick, snare, hats, bell, steel and 808.
- **Above them:** the three approved Cathedral Experiment instruments, played and not modified:
  - THRONE//VOLT, the lead;
  - CATHEDRAL//ENGINE, the organ;
  - HEAVEN//MASS, the choir.
- **Its identity:** Concrete Gospel V3's (the lead's call and moan, the organ answering, the choir as the payoff).

There was one serious composition pass and one bounded QA/fix pass. **Work stops here for the owner's listening review.**

**Nobody has listened yet.** The composer is an AI agent and cannot hear audio. Every musical statement below is intent;
every number is a signal measurement. None of it says the music works; only listening can.

### The opening

The record's identity arrives in the first second, but its payoff waits.

- **Bar 1:** the bell, then THRONE//VOLT's call alone: Ab3 bending up to C4, dark and close.
- **Bar 2:** the organ answers (the Swell, Fm9, box closed).
- **Bar 3:** the lead answers: Eb4 leaping down to Ab3, then the moan to F3.
- **Bar 4:** the choir answers, a hum (Db → C).
- **Bars 5–8:** the conversation again, a step further. Footsteps on the hats, C7♭9, then half a beat of nothing before the drop.

Held back for later: the high choir, the drums, any major chord, the 32' pedal.

### The second hook

It is new, and it is a conversation, not a riff:

- **The lead calls** F4–Ab4–C5 on the 3+3+2 sixteenths. It is the main hook's falling moan turned upside down.
- **The choir answers** Eb–Db–C, slurred. These are V2's original answer notes, reborn as a sung reply.
  - Over Eb it closes on Ab/Eb, a gospel plagal colour.
  - Over C it is C7(♯9 ♭9), the shout.
- **It repeats the same notes over moving harmony**, Db → Fm.
- **Chorus 1:** the third call reaches Db5 and the fourth Eb5.
- **Chorus 2:** the full choir sings the answer while the lead calls with the first hook.

### The ending

- **The resolution is delayed:** the choir sings Db (a deceptive light), then C7sus4 → C7♭9, then **F major** at 2:03, with the full organ, the Pedal's 32' and octavists on F1.
- **The lead holds its Ab3** through all of it and **bends it up to A** as F major arrives. The record's first gesture (Ab bending up to C) is answered by its last (Ab bending up to A).

## What is new against the V3 sketch

V3 is the foundation, not a loop.

- **Reused note for note (V2-approved material):** the hook section's drums, 808 and lead, and the outro's drums and 808.
- **New material:**
  - **The intro's three-way conversation.**
  - **The second hook,** with its choral answer.
  - **The verse 808 line, pocket and lead tags,** leaving room for a voice.
  - **The chorus pocket,** with triplet hats.
  - **Verse 2's pressure pocket, its floor and its stop-time.**
  - **The a-cappella bridge,** with the Neapolitan chord and the organ's Pedal.
  - **Chorus 2:** both hooks at once.
  - **The delayed resolution and the Ab → A bend.**
- **Change of direction:** V3's quick major ending is replaced by a minor amen that is held over, then resolved.

## Mix and low end

- **The drums are V2's own hits.** The same three-layer kick and snare, the same gains, effects and master chain, and V2's 808 duck (−14 dB on every kick). V2's own bars are copied note for note from its source.
- **Low-end discipline:**
  - While the 808 plays, no choir note is below Db3 and the octavists are silent.
  - The organ's Pedal plays only where there is no 808: the bridge and the end.
  - The organ and choir step aside briefly on every kick and snare.
  - The Naves' tails are high-passed at 200–220 Hz.
- **Lead and choir separation (V3's lesson, built in):** the choir's ceiling sits 5–8 dB back under every lead call and comes forward in its own answers.
- **The lead's track:** V3's chain plus a fast peak guard (a second compressor, −8 dB threshold, 1 ms attack). V3's fix pass had found accent spikes that pass the main compressor's 12 ms attack.

### What the pass found and fixed

| # | Found in the candidate | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The choir's arc ran backwards.** Chorus 1's "few singers" (−17.1 LUFS) and the bridge (−16.2) were louder than chorus 2's full choir (−18.4): the payoff was spent early | Chorus 1 −2 dB, the bridge −3 dB. Chorus 2 and the outro +2 dB, with the dips under the lead's calls 2 dB deeper so the calls are unchanged |
| F2 | **The bridge's cry was covered** (−4.0 dB under the rest of the mix) | The lead +1.5 dB in the bridge; under the cry, the choir 4 dB back and the organ 3 dB |
| F3 | **Verse 2 left no room for a voice** (melodic parts +1.8 dB over the beat in the vocal band, busy 98 %) | Its choir bed −8 dB, the Swell −6 dB, the lead's tags −1 dB, verse 1's bed −3 dB |
| F4 | **The closing gesture was hidden.** The organ doubled the lead's held Ab3 and its final A3 in unison, and the lead sat −4.2 dB under the ending's chorale | The organ leaves those two pitches to the lead (a voicing change in two chords); the lead +3 dB from beat 286.5; the choir and Swell 3 dB further back under the last answer |

**Notes on the pass:**

- **Order of work.** Each fix was sized first by simulating the gain changes on the candidate's stems, then built once.
- **What was re-rendered.** The build caches rack renders by score: only the two organ divisions whose voicing changed
  were re-rendered, and six of the eight rack scores were reused.
- **Lead gains.** The lead's level changes are written at twice their size, because its track gain feeds a 2:1
  compressor.

**Remaining trade-offs, left for listening:**

- **Chorus 2's kick is 0.6 dB less proud than V2's slam** (11.8 against 12.4 dB). This is the cost of the full choir
  answering in it.
- **The ending:** the lead stays under the rest of the mix as a whole (−2.8 / −1.3 dB). It is the inner voice of the
  final chorale by design. Fix F4 makes its held Ab and the bend to A clear of unisons, not louder than the choir.

  - FLAC is lossless: each stem is checked by decoding it back to the identical PCM16. The seven WAVs would be 163 MB;
    the FLACs are 31 MB. After a build, the WAVs are in `build/out/stems/`.
- **Editable source:**
  - [`compose.ts`](compose.ts) holds every drum hit, note, control curve, level and ride.
  - [`source/`](source) holds the generated rack scores and the engine project.
  - `delivery/source/concrete-gospel-full-length.mid` holds every part as MIDI, with the drums on channel 10. Bends,
    falls, vowels and sound are not in the MIDI.

## Things only listening can decide

1. **Replay value.** Does it hold attention for 2:12 as an instrumental?
   - Do the verses feel like space waiting for a voice, or like empty space?
2. **The second hook.**
   - Is F–Ab–C answered by a sung Eb–Db–C memorable?
   - Does the hand-over in chorus 2 (the lead on the first hook, the choir on the second) feel like a payoff?
3. **The opening.**
   - Does the lead–organ–lead–choir conversation set the identity in the first seconds?
   - Is the drop at 0:13 earned?
4. **The bridge.** Is the a-cappella Neapolitan valley the emotional centre, or a stall?
5. **The ending.**
   - Does the held Ab bending to A over F major land as the release the record has waited for?
   - Or is the delayed resolution (Db, then C7) one step too long?
6. **The trade-offs above:** chorus 2's kick, and the lead inside the final chorale.

```bash
PYTHON=python3 node experiments/concrete-gospel-004/deliver.ts
```

- `build.ts` verifies the V2 backing (and rebuilds it with V2's own commands the first time). It then renders the eight
  rack scores four at a time, reusing any whose score is unchanged, renders the engine project, and writes the record
  and stems.
- `deliver.ts` runs the final QA and the freeze check, encodes and verifies the FLAC stems, writes the MIDI and fills
  `delivery/`.
- The sources pass `tsc` with the repository's strict settings, checked with a temporary config that is not committed.
